# Stage 1: Planning the deck

Act as the art director and information designer. The output is a DECK STYLE GUIDE plus one
SLIDE SPEC per slide. Each spec is rendered as exactly one finished 16:9 image, so it has to
be complete on its own.

## Step 1: Angle, structure and content (Checkpoint 1)

**1a. Read everything** in the conversation and every uploaded file. Inventory the user's assets
(photos, logos, charts, data tables, diagrams) and decide which slide each one serves. Real assets
beat invented imagery when they carry the argument. If the user gave only a short topic prompt or
brief notes without source documents, synthesize authoritative, domain-accurate mechanisms, concrete
case examples, and real benchmarks/statistics for the topic (using web search if available and
needed for factual accuracy).

**1b. Pick the angle.** Don't summarize everything. Choose one focused thread with a point of
view that the material supports strongly, and state it as a reframe (e.g. turning narrative or
biographical material into an applied decision framework, analyzing historical records as an
operational network, isolating how biological structures act as sensors, or testing one core
question across competing schools of thought). If the material is broad, offer 2–3 candidate angles
in Checkpoint 1 and recommend one. If the user gave a focus, use theirs.

**1c. Identify the deck's intent and genre**, because they decide density and the ending:
- *Explain a story/history/literature* → narrative arc, evidence quotes, closing quote or emblem.
- *Explain ideas/compare thinkers or options* → a question asked across the series, a synthesis
  timeline or matrix, closing reflective questions.
- *Teach how to do something* → numbered steps with claim titles, a diagnostic table, closing
  action checklist.
- *Brief technical/research results* → paradigm shift → system → results with real numbers →
  future work, optional appendix with data tables/charts.

**1d. Choose a series formula** for the middle of the deck: "Step 1–N", "Case 01–N", "<Concept> is
___.", "Mechanism: ___", or structured subject profiles. Every member uses the same title pattern
and layout skeleton. Where depth matters, pair a "who/what" slide with a "how" slide (profile or
case → map, cross-section, schematic or timeline).

**1e. Plan 10–15 slides** (or the user's count): Cover → framing → series/sections → synthesis
(matrix, timeline, ripple diagram, two-lens summary) → intent-matched closing.

Output for Checkpoint 1: the angle (and alternatives if any), thesis, asset map, and a TOC with
number, working title, purpose, and key content per slide.

## Step 2: Design the master theme from scratch (Checkpoint 2)
There are no presets. Invent an art direction for this deck only.

**2a. Mine the content for visual raw material.** Write down, briefly:
- Subject, domain, and the genre of the sources (letters, journalism, fiction, academic book,
  research paper, interviews, internal docs).
- Era and place, if any.
- The physical "stuff" of the angle: its documents, tools, instruments, materials, environments
  (ledgers, wax seals, circuit boards, sea charts, lab glassware, X-rays, graph paper…).
- Tone the audience should feel (urgent, reverent, playful, forensic, optimistic) and the setting
  (boardroom, classroom, conference keynote, self-study).
- Any brand constraints from the user (colors, logo, fonts) — these override everything else.

**2b. Pick the theme family** that fits the intent and genre (invent a fresh, topic-native color
palette, surface texture, and motif set for the user's topic while following these structural and
media grammars):
- *Immersive world* (story, history, biography, literature): a physical world with period or
  contextual props (archival dossier, illuminated manuscript, investigation board, situation-room
  briefing, cultural artifact). Grammar: choose either a **dark atmospheric polarity** (a deep,
  low-luminance ground drawn from the subject's world paired with warm or crisp high-luminance
  floating cards with `10–12px` radius, soft shadow, `~4%` internal padding, and low-opacity
  `~10–15%` geometric/network lines or architectural panels in the margins) OR a **tactile
  substrate polarity** (full-bleed aged paper, vellum, parchment, or fabric canvas with dark period
  ink and ornate or ruled frames). Render portraits and historical/narrative artifacts as
  ground-tinted monochrome/duotone engravings or period illustrations blending softly into the
  background (or set in framed panels), paired with fine metallic or ink line-engravings for
  seals/emblems. Pair a high-contrast transitional or old-style display serif (titles and huge
  ALL-CAPS profile names) with a clean neutral sans-serif (dossier bullets) and italic serif/sans
  for quotes preceded by a large accent open-quote glyph (`“`).
- *Field/specimen editorial* (popular science, nature, hardware, product anatomy): airy warm
  field-journal or laboratory-clean ground with faint (`~10–15%`) topographic, contour, or schematic
  line art; crisp surface cards with soft shadows and a solid colored top tab bar encoding each
  thematic pillar or category. Combine macro photoreal subjects or 3D cutaway illustrations with
  thin technical vector overlays (dashed leader lines ending in dots, thin-bordered callout boxes,
  zoom circles) and small pen-and-ink or technical engravings in tables/cards. Book or editorial
  serif for centered `Hook: Clarifier` titles; neutral sans for lead paragraphs and bold run-in
  lead-ins (`The Mechanism:`, `The Evidence:`).
- *Genre-native editorial* (business, leadership, strategy, how-to, playbooks): tactile
  architectural, newsprint, or studio paper ground (such as warm drafting stock covered
  edge-to-edge with a subtle uniform `1px` square engineering graph-paper grid or fine editorial
  ruling). Strictly flat 2D vector diagrams (funnels, exponential activation curves, seesaws,
  floor-plan schematics, concentric circles) using a 4-role editorial palette derived from the
  topic: deep primary ink + bold structural accent + neutral structural grey + a high-luminance
  marker/highlighter tint — zero stock photos or 3D shading. Heavy high-contrast editorial serif
  for left-aligned full-sentence claim titles and italic pull quotes; geometric sans-serif for bold
  ALL-CAPS diagram labels and attributions; high-contrast marker/highlighter swipes behind the
  decisive 3–6 words of each quote or output badge.
- *Source-native technical* (research, benchmarks, engineering, clinical/AI/data): clean
  publication-grade canvas (warm scientific paper or crisp technical off-white) with delicate,
  low-opacity (`~15–18%`) domain-derived background linework in the corners (flowing neural/branch
  pathways, circuit traces, molecular lattices, or wave contours). One geometric sans-serif family
  across all weights: left-aligned bold title (`~5.5%` height) + 1–3-sentence regular subtitle
  (`~2.8%` height) stating the quantitative takeaway. Strict semantic color encoding across all
  charts and callouts (a high-salience focal accent for the proposed system/finding vs. a muted
  contrasting tone for prior SOTA/baseline), pairing crisp rounded chart cards (`10px` radius, `1px`
  border, direct bold value labels above bars) with stacked metric callout cards led by huge bold
  accent stats.
- *Per-section shift* (modifier): when the deck compares distinct worlds, eras or thinkers, each
  section can take its subject's visual language. Then lock the layout skeleton, the title/body
  fonts, and the signature device so it still reads as one deck.

**2c. Generate 2–3 distinct directions** within or across families. Make them genuinely different
(light vs dark, photographic vs illustrated vs flat, period vs contemporary). Avoid generic
corporate looks and themes you've used before. Pick the one that best makes the angle visible.

**2d. Write the DECK STYLE GUIDE** for the chosen direction (start the block with plain `DECK STYLE GUIDE` — do not put the deck title in double quotes on that header line, or the renderer may print it as a running header on content slides):
1. **CONCEPT**: one or two sentences naming the family, the world/metaphor, and the mood.
2. **PALETTE**: invent 5–7 colors freshly derived from this topic's materials, era, and mood—never
   reuse a generic or prior deck's palette—and lock each color to an explicit HEX code and semantic
   role (ground, card surface, primary ink, muted ink, and semantic accents such as one color per
   pillar, conflict vs. alliance, a luminous highlighter tint, or baseline vs. proposed system).
   Verify high luminance contrast between text and its surface/ground.
3. **TYPOGRAPHY**: a display face and a body face, plus at most one utility face with a single job
   (e.g. typewriter mono for evidence). Name real fonts *and* describe their visual traits without
   putting font names in double quotes (e.g. `high-contrast transitional serif in the style of
   Playfair Display, sharp serifs`), because `TEXT RULES` reserves double quotes for rendered slide
   text. Say which face is used for which role, including inside series slides. Relative sizes:
   title ≈ 5–7% of slide height, body ≈ 2.6–3.2%, captions ≈ 2%. Use fonts that support the deck's
   language.
4. **BACKGROUND & TEXTURE**: e.g. paper grain, faint grid, topographic lines, blueprint drawings.
   Specify that the background fills the 16:9 canvas full-bleed edge-to-edge.
5. **IMAGERY**: one picture style (or, for per-section shifts, one style per section, listed),
   e.g. "photoreal props on navy with pale technical line drawings", "flat two-tone vector, no
   photos". Say how the user's own photos are treated to fit.
6. **SIGNATURE DEVICE**: the one element that appears on every slide, described precisely enough
   to stay identical (e.g. a recurring corner emblem with a fixed motif, color, and size; a single
   connector thread linking evidence across the slide; a fixed evaluative sidebar; or a high-contrast
   highlighter swipe behind key phrases). Never vary its details between slides.
7. **COMPONENT KIT**: the exact look of the content card, quote box (and attribution format, keeping
   surrounding quotation marks even when a decorative quote glyph is present), callout/label,
   section divider, table (specify internal container padding so thin grid lines stop cleanly short
   of card edges or outer margins, with uniform divider stroke weights), chart, diagram nodes/lines
   (with explicit stroke weights), and the series card, including border, radius, shadow and fill.
8. **RECURRING MOTIFS** (optional): 1–2 secondary threads (bookend image, color tab per pillar).

## Step 3: Write one SLIDE SPEC per slide (Checkpoint 3), in exactly this format

```
SLIDE {n}/{N} — {ARCHETYPE}
PURPOSE: one line on what the viewer should understand.
TEXT TO RENDER (verbatim, nothing else):
  {Role}: "{exact text}"
  ...
HERO VISUAL: concrete description of the single dominant image or diagram. It must ARGUE the
  point (metaphor, mechanism, data), not decorate.
COMPOSITION: where each block sits on a simple grid (left 40% / right 60%, top band, etc.),
  alignment, margins, what is largest, and where the signature device sits.
ASSETS: (optional) which uploaded file is used and how (placement, crop, treatment).
STYLE NOTES: (optional) only slide-specific deviations from the style guide.
```

## Rules for the specs
- **Titles**: a two-part hook ("Hook: Clarifier"), a series formula ("Step 2: ..." or "Case 02: ..."),
  or, for how-to and argument decks, a full-sentence claim that states the takeaway.
- **Density by intent**: narrative decks get ONE paragraph of 30–70 words; how-to decks a claim
  title + 1–2 quotes + a diagram; technical decks a 1–3-sentence subtitle + chart/stats. Bullets max
  4, each ≤ 25 words. Total on-slide text ≤ 110 words (≤ 140 for technical/appendix slides;
  cover ≤ 25, divider ≤ 50).
- **Quotes & Evidence**: when the sources contain primary quotes, render them verbatim with
  attribution ("— Name, Month Year" or "— Name, Title, Organization"). Never invent fake quotes,
  numbers, or names. When the user provides only a topic or notes without verbatim quotes, use real
  verifiable historical/published quotes if known, or replace quote boxes with structured evidence
  components (metric callouts, case-study cards, or mechanism callouts with bold run-in lead-ins). A
  closing takeaway line that isn't a real quote must never be set in quotation marks.
- **Charts**: give every value, label, axis title and legend entry, and which color is which series.
  When a chart sits in the left column with a rotated vertical Y-axis label, place the vertical
  Y-axis line at ~12% from the left canvas edge so the rotated label sits well inside the 6–7%
  outer margin and never clips.
  **Diagrams**: list every node label and every connection, state explicitly whether each label sits
  inside its node shape or outside to the left/right, and specify thin, uniform stroke weights for
  nodes, connectors, and dividers.
- **Outer margins**: always state an explicit 6–7% outer safe margin on all four sides in
  COMPOSITION (e.g. "Top band spanning from 7% left margin to 93% right margin") rather than
  "full-width", which can cause long titles or left-side axes to clip at the canvas edge.
- **Series slides** repeat the same layout and the same type roles. Always restate the visual font
  description inline on key fields in each series spec (e.g. `Heading (bold high-contrast serif ALL
  CAPS — never sans-serif)`) rather than relying only on "Same as Slide 3", so typography never
  drifts across turns.
- **Never put renderer-facing words in TEXT TO RENDER**: no field names ("Title:", "The Visual",
  "The Evidence"), font names, hex codes or instructions. Use neutral role names in the spec
  (Title, Body, Quote, Label-1…). If a component needs a visible heading, write that heading as a
  quoted string on purpose.
- **Each string appears once.** Don't repeat the title as a second headline unless the spec says so.
- Vary the archetype outside the series; don't use the same layout three times in a row otherwise.
- Use the user's language for on-slide text if they write in another language, but keep the spec labels in English.

## Core archetype composition blueprints (use these spatial grids in Step 3)
- **Cover**: Full-bleed textured or atmospheric ground; title (`~8–10%` height) and subtitle (`~3.5%` height) stacked at `~35%` height or left-aligned in the upper-left quadrant; a single iconic hero visual (silhouette horizon arc, editorial vector emblem, macro specimen, or photoreal artifact) anchored cleanly in the lower or right half with at least `35%` open breathing room.
- **Framing / Paradigm Split**: Top title + concise framing lead; below, a two-panel or left/right contrast (`Old Paradigm / Baseline` in muted grey/slate on the left vs. `New Paradigm / Reframe` in primary accent on the right) connected by a directional transition arrow or fulcrum.
- **Section / Pillar Divider**: Full-bleed schematic or contour line-art background matched to the pillar's theme; a centered floating surface card (`~55%` width) topped with a solid colored tab bar in that pillar's accent color, holding the section title in display type and a centered 2–3-line framing paragraph.
- **Roster / Overview Grid**: Left `~26%` column holds a 2-line display title at top + 1–2 left-aligned framing paragraphs below; Right `~68%` holds a multi-row grid of vertical cards (e.g. 4 top + 3 bottom left-aligned), each with a circular duotone/engraved portrait medallion in the upper two-thirds, bold serif name centered below, and accent sans-serif epithet beneath, linked behind the cards by thin straight network lines.
- **Profile / Dossier Split**: Left `~48%` holds a full-height duotone/engraved portrait blending into the ground (or a framed portrait panel occupying the left `~40%`) with the subject's name overlaid on the lower-left in huge (`~11%` height) bold high-contrast serif ALL CAPS; Right `~46–50%` holds a floating rounded dossier card (`12px` radius, `10%` top/bottom margin) with a medium-bold role header (`Role 1 | Role 2`), thin accent hairline rule, up to 4 hyphen-prefixed sans-serif bullets with hanging indents, and at the bottom a large accent open-quote glyph (`“`) on the left followed by the italic quote enclosed in double quotation marks.
- **Step Slide (Claim Title + Flat Vector Diagram + Evidence Quotes)**: Strict `7%` outer safe margin on all four sides. Top band (`7%` to `93%` width) holds a 2-line left-aligned full-sentence editorial serif claim title with a thin horizontal rule beneath. Below the rule, a thin vertical divider splits Left `~44–48%` (a flat 2D vector mechanism diagram: 3-stage funnel, exponential activation curve with vertical Y-axis indented to `~12%` from the left canvas edge and a shaded highlighter target zone, seesaw, or cycle) and Right `~48–52%` (2–3 stacked attributed quote blocks or contrasting light/dark quote cards with neon highlighter swipes behind key phrases).
- **Annotated Specimen / Mechanism Cutaway / Map**: Centered or top-left title + 2-line lead paragraph; main canvas holds a macro specimen photo, 3D technical cutaway, or clean cartographic map occupying `~55–65%` width, paired with 3 structured callout boxes (thin accent border, `6px` radius, bold heading + 2-line explanation) connected to exact features on the visual via thin dashed or solid leader lines ending in a small dot.
- **Comparison Table**: Title top-left + 2-line intro paragraph below spanning `~88%` width; lower `~62%` holds a floating rounded table card (`~5.5%` outer margin on left, right, and bottom) with `~2.5%` internal padding on all 4 sides so thin (`1.5px`) horizontal and vertical grid lines stop cleanly short of the card's rounded edges. Both vertical dividers share the exact same thin (`1.5px`) weight; Column 1 (`~22%` width) holds centered bold sans-serif row labels; Columns 2–3 (`~39%` width each) hold centered regular sans-serif cells under color-coded bold serif ALL-CAPS headers.
- **Relationship / Network Diagram**: Title centered at top; centered large rounded card (`~75%` width and height) containing an asymmetric network of large thin (`2px` stroke) circles connected by straight perimeter lines (including diagonals). State for every node whether its heading and pipe-separated (`|`) member list sit centered inside the circle, overlapping its left arc with card-colored backing, or outside to the right of the circle; mark internal conflict by splitting that node's circle down the middle with a thin (`2px`) jagged accent fault line with opposing labels in the left and right halves.
- **Data Chart + Metric Callouts**: Strict `7%` outer margin on all four sides. Top band holds a left-aligned bold geometric sans title + 2-line subtitle (`~86%` width). Below: Left `~58%` holds a crisp rounded chart card (`10px` radius, `1px` border, internal padding) with a top-right two-item color legend and a grouped bar chart encoding proposed vs. baseline series by color, with exact bold percentage/value labels printed directly above every bar; Right `~36%` holds two stacked rounded metric callout cards, each led by a huge bold accent stat (e.g. a headline percentage or `+X%` delta), bold heading, and concise explanation.
- **Closing Synthesis / Emblem**: A single large centered rounded card (`~84%` width × `~86%` height) over the textured background; accent serif title centered near the top, 3-line synthesis body paragraph centered beneath in clean sans-serif, fine line-engraved emblem/seal centered in the middle (`~42%` of card height), and a 2-line medium-italic closing takeaway centered near the bottom.
- **Closing Action Checklist / Reflective Questions**: Top synthesis claim or framing header; below, 3–4 horizontal numbered action cards or pillar question panels arranged in a clean grid, each with a bold action/question headline, a high-contrast badge or check/number marker, and 1–2 lines of concrete execution guidance.
