# Design DNA: why NotebookLM decks look premium

Drawn from 9 NotebookLM decks (~120 slides) across history, literature, natural science,
philosophy, business and medical AI. The decks named below only illustrate the principles. Do not
reuse their themes; design a new one for every deck (see `planner.md` step 2).

1. **One focused, reframed angle.** A deck is a narrow selection with a point of view, never a
   summary of everything: one paper out of 16, women *spies* out of "women of the Revolution",
   animal *cognition* out of 65 science stories, Holmes stories reframed as "an applied analytical
   framework". The title states the reframe.
2. **The art direction comes from the angle and the genre of the content.** Four families seen:
   - *Immersive world* for history, literature and narrative: a physical world with props
     (archival dossier, illuminated manuscript, detective's case board, spy file).
   - *Field/specimen editorial* for popular science: photoreal subjects with thin technical annotation.
   - *Genre-native editorial* for business and how-to: flat vector diagrams on grid paper,
     claim titles, one highlighter accent, no photos.
   - *Source-native technical* for research and engineering: the look of the field's own figures,
     real charts, color that encodes data, an appendix.
   - Modifier: *per-section style shifts* when the deck compares distinct worlds or eras. Each
     section borrows its subject's visual language; a fixed layout skeleton keeps it one deck.
3. **One signature device on every slide.** A red thread that connects evidence, a wax seal, a
   fixed sidebar ("The Freedom Test"), a highlighter mark, flowing branch lines, a gold ribbon.
   Defined once, identical everywhere.
4. **Every content slide has one hero visual that *is* the argument.** Never decorative stock
   art: a balance scale for leverage, a seesaw for "managing the business → managing the people", a
   river cross-section for a dangerous crossing, a funnel for focus, a tree bleeding from its roots
   for inherited guilt.
5. **Mixed media where the family allows it.** Photoreal or engraved imagery with crisp vector
   annotation on top (leader lines, callouts, zoom circles, arrows on maps, pinned notes).
6. **Reading deck, not keynote.** Title (a hook, a formula title, or a full-sentence claim) + a
   short paragraph + hero visual + one evidence component (attributed primary quote, chart with
   real numbers, table, or callouts). Technical decks run denser; how-to decks use short claims.
7. **A series formula drives the middle.** "Step 1–N", "Case File 01–04", "Freedom is ___.",
   "System Function: ___", person profiles. Same title pattern and layout skeleton for every member.
   Pair "who/what" slides with "how" slides (case file → map / schematic / timeline).
8. **A small, strict component kit.** Quote box, content card, callout, divider, table, chart,
   diagram nodes. Same border, radius, shadow and type every time.
9. **Two typefaces, plus at most one utility face with a single job** (typewriter for evidence,
   condensed caps for display). Italic for quotes.
10. **Restrained palette with meaning.** One ground, one surface, one ink, 1–3 accents that encode
    something (red = threat/Britain, blue = baseline vs orange = new model, one color per pillar).
11. **Generous margins (~5–6% of width), a clear grid, high contrast, one focal point per slide.**
12. **Narrative arc with an intent-matched ending.** Cover → framing → series/sections →
    synthesis (matrix, timeline, ripple diagram) → closing that fits the intent: quote or emblem
    (history), reflective questions (ideas), action checklist (how-to), future directions +
    appendix (technical).
13. **The layout changes almost every slide; the visual language never does** (except deliberate
    per-section shifts, which keep the layout skeleton instead).

## Layout archetypes seen
Cover · framing question · roster grid · profile split (portrait + dossier card) · case-file card
(field list + silhouette) · two-column compare / old-vs-new paradigm · comparison matrix ·
relationship/network diagram · process steps / cycle · step slide (claim title + diagram + quotes)
· annotated map · cross-section · schematic/floor plan · timeline · data chart + stats ·
anatomy/zoom-callout figure · ripple/concentric diagram · section divider · full-bleed scene with
caption · triptych · action checklist · reflective questions · appendix table/chart · closing quote/emblem.
